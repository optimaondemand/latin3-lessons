⏰ PART 1 · THE TEXT 14 min

Adapted from Prō Archiā Poētā

Cicero defends the citizenship of the poet Archias. Read each line aloud, find the relative clauses, and name their type as you go. The gloss below carries the harder words and constructions.

1Quis est quī nōn videat quantum mōmentum habeant studia hūmānitātis? 2Archiās est is quī ingeniō omnēs superāre videātur et doctrīnā. 3Nōn sunt multī quī hanc viam virtūtis et laudis inīre velint. 4Pompēius lēgātōs mīsit quī Archiam rogārent ut dē bellīs eius scrīberet. 5Hūmānitātis studiīs sumus ērūdītī — sīne hīs nēmō est quī satis valeat. 6Poēta est is quī immortālitātem per carmina rebus gestīs tribuit. 7Cicerō erat quī et dīcere et scrībere et bene vīvere posset — rārus homo. 8Mīsimus libellum ad iūdicēs quī causam nostram cognōscerent et probārent.
Gloss: quantum mōmentum — how great the weight/importance (indirect question) · studia hūmānitātis — the pursuits of culture/civilization · doctrīnā — learning, scholarship (abl.) · viam inīre — to enter upon a road/path · hūmānitātis studiīs — in the liberal studies · ērūdītī sumus — we have been educated (perf. passive) · carmina — songs, poems · rebus gestīs — to deeds accomplished (dative after tribuit) · tribuit — attributes, gives · libellum — a little book, a brief written document · cognōscerent et probārent — might examine and approve (purpose)
📚 PART 2 · LITERARY NOTE 8 min
🏛️ PRŌ ARCHIĀ AND THE DEFENSE OF CULTURE

Cicero delivered Prō Archiā in 62 BC to defend the poet Archias's Roman citizenship. The speech transcends the legal case: it becomes a meditation on why a civilized state needs poets, historians, and scholars.

The relative clauses of characteristic (quis est quī, nēmō est quī, is est quī) are Cicero's argumentative tools — they define what kind of person matters, what kind of state flourishes, what kind of legacy endures. Recognize them: this is Cicero at his most philosophically ambitious.

In your own words: how do Cicero's relative clauses of characteristic (“there is no one who…”, “he is the sort who…”) help him argue for the value of poetry? Think it through, then reveal one way to say it.

By phrasing his claims as characteristics of a type rather than facts about one person, Cicero makes them feel universal and self-evident: “there is no one who does not see…” forces the jury to agree, and “a poet is the sort of person who confers immortality” defines the very worth of the poet he defends. The subjunctive turns opinion into what seems the nature of things.
📌 PART 4 · COMMIT, THEN REVEAL 10 min

Translate three lines

Draft a full translation of each line in your notebook before revealing. Then compare — the note after each explains the construction.

1. Quis est quī nōn videat quantum mōmentum habeant studia hūmānitātis?

“Who is there who does not see how great an importance the pursuits of culture have?” — quis est quī + subjunctive (videat) = characteristic clause; quantum mōmentum habeant = indirect question (Module 06 review) embedded within.

2. Pompēius lēgātōs mīsit quī Archiam rogārent ut dē bellīs eius scrīberet.

“Pompey sent ambassadors to ask Archias that he should write about his wars.” — quī rogārent = relative clause of purpose (mīsit + quī + subj.); ut…scrīberet = indirect command nested inside the purpose clause.

3. Poēta est is quī immortālitātem per carmina rebus gestīs tribuit.

“A poet is the sort of person who gives immortality to deeds through songs.” — is quī defines what type of person a poet is. Note: tribuit here is present indicative (a specific true claim), which some editors accept in this pattern; watch for such variation in Cicero.

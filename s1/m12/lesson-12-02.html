★ Two Subjunctive Relative Clauses
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Yesterday you felt the shift from fact to characterization. Today you name the two clauses that make it — and learn the signals that flag each.
A subjunctive in a relative clause can mean two different things this module. The relative clause of characteristic describes what sort of person or thing the antecedent is. The relative clause of purpose tells you why someone was sent, chosen, or provided. Each announces itself with recognizable triggers. Learn the signals well and you will parse Cicero's arguments on sight.

📚 PART 2 · THE TWO TYPES 16 min

Characteristic and purpose

① RELATIVE CLAUSE OF CHARACTERISTIC

Describes what kind of person or thing the antecedent is by nature. The antecedent is often indefinite or a general type. Trigger words: is est quī, nēmō est quī, nōn est quī, sunt quī, quis est quī. Always subjunctive.

Translate: “He is the sort of man who…” / “There is no one who…” / “There are those who…”

② RELATIVE CLAUSE OF PURPOSE

Expresses the purpose for which someone or something is sent, chosen, or provided. Often follows verbs of sending, choosing, and appointing. The relative pronoun is equivalent to ut + pronoun. Always subjunctive.

Translate: “…who would… / in order to…” — e.g. Lēgātōs mīsit quī pācem peterent, “He sent ambassadors to seek peace / who would seek peace.”

Set all three side by side and the system is clear:

TypeSignalMoodTranslation
Ordinary relativequī + indicativeIndicative“who (in fact) does…”
Characteristicis/nēmō/nōn est quī + subj.Subjunctive“the sort who… / no one who…”
Purposeverb of sending/choosing + quī + subj.Subjunctive“who would… / in order to…”
📌 PART 3 · THE DIAGNOSTIC 6 min

A three-question test

💡 INDICATIVE OR SUBJUNCTIVE — AND WHICH KIND?

1. Is the antecedent a specific, known individual? → ordinary relative, indicative.

2. Is the antecedent a general type or indefinite person (watch for nēmō est quī, is est quī)? → characteristic, subjunctive.

3. Does the clause express why the antecedent was sent or chosen (verb of sending before the quī)? → purpose, subjunctive.

✅ TRY IT · the signal
A verb of sending or choosing standing just before a quī + subjunctive most often signals which type?

Commit first, then reveal. Classify Lēgātōs mīsit quī pācem peterent and give the reasoning behind your choice. Say it aloud, then reveal.

Relative clause of purpose. The main verb mīsit (“he sent”) is a verb of sending, and the quī + subjunctive peterent is equivalent to ut eī peterent. Translate: “He sent ambassadors to seek peace” / “who would seek peace.”

Lesson 12.03
★ The Words of Culture, Then the Clauses
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
You have the grammar. Today you gather the words Cicero uses to argue for culture — then apply the two clause types at sentence level.
The eight words of Module 12 are the vocabulary of Cicero's Prō Archiā: poēta, glōria, ingēnium, hūmānitās, and the rest. They are the terms in which Rome debated what makes a person — and a state — worthy. First you learn them; then you classify relative clauses in full sentences, sharpening the diagnostic before tomorrow's reading.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to give the meaning of the eight Module 12 vocabulary words, with genitive and gender.
2
I will be able to classify relative clauses (ordinary / characteristic / purpose) in full Latin sentences and justify each choice.
📚 PART 1 · VOCABULARY 12 min

Module 12 words — poetry, humanity, glory

Read each aloud with its genitive and gender. These are abstract nouns of value — the currency of Cicero's argument.

LatinMeaning
poēta, -ae (m.)poet
glōria, -ae (f.)glory, fame, renown
ingēnium, -ī (n.)natural talent, genius, mind
hūmānitās, -ātis (f.)humanity, culture, refinement
laus, laudis (f.)praise, glory, distinction
testis, -is (m./f.)witness
cōnsuētūdō, -inis (f.)custom, practice, habit
dignitās, -ātis (f.)dignity, worth, standing
💬 ENGLISH FROM THESE ROOTS

Ingēnium gives us ingenious and engine; hūmānitās stands behind the humanities; dignitās yields dignity and dignitary; testis underlies testimony and testify. Cicero's vocabulary of worth is still the vocabulary of our own.

✅ PART 3 · CLASSIFY IN CONTEXT 18 min

Ordinary, characteristic, or purpose?

Now the sentences carry the module's vocabulary. Run yesterday's diagnostic on each. Five reps — wrong answers give a hint; use Reset to try again.

✅ TRY IT · classify
Poēta quem amāmus clārus est. What type of relative clause?
✅ TRY IT · classify
Nēmō est quī hanc glōriam nōn cupiat. What type?
✅ TRY IT · classify
Pompeius lēgātōs mīsit quī poētam laudārent. What type?
✅ TRY IT · classify
Is est quī dignitātem semper servēt. What type?
✅ TRY IT · classify
Hūmānitās quam cōluit omnēs movēbat. (“The culture that he cultivated moved everyone.”) What type?

Commit first, then reveal. Classify Sunt quī laudem magis quam hūmānitātem cupiant and translate it. Say your answer aloud, then reveal.

Relative clause of characteristic. Sunt quī (“there are those who…”) is a characteristic trigger, and cupiant is present subjunctive. Translate: “There are those who desire praise more than culture.” The clause describes a general type of person, not one named individual.

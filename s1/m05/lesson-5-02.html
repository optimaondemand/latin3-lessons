★ Two Stems, Two Suffixes — -erim and -issem
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Both new tenses are built on ground you already own: the perfect stem. Learn two suffix sets and you have them.
The perfect active subjunctive and the pluperfect active subjunctive are the most mechanical forms in the whole subjunctive system. Each starts from the perfect stem (your third principal part, minus the final -ī) and adds a fixed suffix — -erim for the perfect, -issem for the pluperfect. There is a memorable shortcut for each. Today you drill the forms cold, so that on Day 3 you can spend your attention on usage, not spelling.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to form the perfect active subjunctive from the perfect stem plus -erim, -erīs, -erit, -erīmus, -erītis, -erint.
2
I will be able to form the pluperfect active subjunctive from the perfect stem plus -issem, -issēs, -isset, -issēmus, -issētis, -issent.
3
I will be able to tell perfect from pluperfect subjunctive on sight by the suffix.
⏰ PART 1 · FIND THE STEM 5 min

Everything starts from the perfect stem

Both new tenses build on the perfect stem — take the third principal part and drop the final -ī. This is the same stem that powers the perfect, pluperfect, and future perfect indicative, so you already know how to find it.

3rd principal partDrop -īPerfect stem
amāvīamāvīamāv-
dūxīdūxīdūx-

📚 PART 2 · THE PERFECT SUBJUNCTIVE 10 min

Perfect active subjunctive: -erim endings

Add the endings -erim, -erīs, -erit, -erīmus, -erītis, -erint to the perfect stem. It is used in a subordinate clause after a primary main verb when the subordinate action happened before the main action.

📌 RULE 1 · PERFECT SUBJUNCTIVE FORMULA

Perfect stem + -erim endings. Example: amāvī → stem amāv- → amāverim, amāverīs, amāverit, amāverīmus, amāverītis, amāverint. Translation: “I may have loved,” or in context: “that I loved / had loved.” Memory hook: the perfect subjunctive looks almost exactly like the future perfect indicative — they differ only in the 1st singular (amāverō indic. vs. amāverim subj.).

amō — perfect subjunctive

amāverimI may have loved
amāverīsyou may have loved
amāverithe/she may have loved
amāverīmuswe may have loved
amāverītisyou all may have loved
amāverintthey may have loved

dūcō — perfect subjunctive

dūxerimI may have led
dūxerīsyou may have led
dūxerithe/she may have led
dūxerīmuswe may have led
dūxerītisyou all may have led
dūxerintthey may have led
📚 PART 3 · THE PLUPERFECT SUBJUNCTIVE 10 min

Pluperfect active subjunctive: -issem endings

Also built on the perfect stem, plus the endings -issem, -issēs, -isset, -issēmus, -issētis, -issent. It is used after a secondary main verb when the subordinate action happened before that secondary main verb.

📌 RULE 2 · PLUPERFECT SUBJUNCTIVE FORMULA

Perfect stem + -issem endings. The stem vowel is the same as the perfect active; only the suffix changes. amāvissem, amāvissēs, amāvisset, amāvissēmus, amāvissētis, amāvissent. Translation in context: “that I had loved,” “(if) I had loved.” Memory hook: the pluperfect subjunctive is the perfect active infinitive (amāvisse) + personal endings.

amō — pluperfect subjunctive

amāvissemI had loved (subj.)
amāvissēsyou had loved
amāvissethe/she had loved
amāvissēmuswe had loved
amāvissētisyou all had loved
amāvissentthey had loved

dūcō — pluperfect subjunctive

dūxissemI had led (subj.)
dūxissēsyou had led
dūxissethe/she had led
dūxissēmuswe had led
dūxissētisyou all had led
dūxissentthey had led
💡 TELL THEM APART AT A GLANCE

The two suffixes never overlap. See -eri- (as in dūxerit) → perfect subjunctive. See -isse- (as in dūxisset) → pluperfect subjunctive. The double -ss- is your surest pluperfect flag.

Out loud: build the full 3rd-singular set for stupeō (perf. stem stupu-) — perfect subjunctive, then pluperfect subjunctive. Say both, then reveal.

Perfect subjunctive 3rd sg. = stupuerit ("he may have been stunned"). Pluperfect subjunctive 3rd sg. = stupuisset ("he had been stunned"). Same stem stupu-; the suffix (-erit vs. -isset) is the only difference.

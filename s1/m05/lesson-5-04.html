⏰ PART 1 · READ THE PASSAGE 14 min

Trīmalchiō Intrat — Petronius, Satyrica 32–33 (adapted)

Read each line aloud. Find the main verb first — that fixes the sequence — then work out each subjunctive clause. Use the gloss only after you have tried a line yourself.

1Cum ad triclinium pervēnissēmus, servī iam omnia parāverant ut nihil deesset.
2Trīmalchiō in pompā suā intrāvit; omnēs mīrātī sunt quī tam superbus esset.
3Hospitēs rogāvērunt num ipse Trīmalchiō vēnisset — nēmō crēdiderat eum tam dīvitem fuisse.
4Ille priusquam respondisset, digitōs concrepuit ut servī accurrerent.
5Interim argentum in mēnsā positum est cuius apparātus ita mīrus erat ut omnēs stupērent.
6Tam superbē incessit ut nēmō eum salūtāre ausus esset.
7Tandem locūtus est et dīxit sē per multōs annōs ēsurīvisse antequam lībertātem cōnsecūtus esset.
8Omnēs hospitēs rīsērunt, quamquam nescīrent cūr rīsissent.
Gloss: triclinium — dining room  ·  deesset — would be lacking (impf. subj. of dēsum)  ·  pompā — in procession / parade  ·  concrepuit — snapped (perfect of concrepō)  ·  accurrerent — would run up (impf. subj., purpose)  ·  mēnsā — table (abl.)  ·  incessit — walked, advanced (perfect of incēdō)  ·  lībertātem cōnsecūtus esset — had obtained his freedom (plupf. subj., deponent cōnsequor)  ·  antequam — before
💡 WATCH THE SEQUENCE AT WORK

Line 1: cum pervēnissēmus (plupf. subj.) — "when we had arrived," a "before" action in secondary sequence. Line 5: ita mīrus…ut omnēs stupērent — a result clause (impf. subj., same-time). Line 3: num…vēnisset — indirect question, "before" action in secondary sequence. Every subjunctive here is a decision you can now make.

📚 PART 2 · THE SATIRIST'S CRAFT 9 min
📜 LITERARY NOTE · PETRONIUS'S NARRATIVE VOICE

Petronius writes through a first-person narrator, Encolpius, who is simultaneously inside the scene and ironically distant from it. Line 8 captures this perfectly: the guests laugh without knowing why (nescīrent cūr rīsissent) — an indirect question with pluperfect subjunctive (secondary sequence). Even the laughter is performed, not genuine. Petronius's satire works by letting characters reveal themselves without authorial comment — Trimalchio's vulgarity, the guests' cowed flattery, the whole hollow theatre of the feast, all shown rather than judged.

Notice how the grammar itself carries the irony. The stacked "before" subjunctives (vēnisset, fuisse, respondisset, cōnsecūtus esset, rīsissent) keep pushing the reader back through layers of prior action — a syntax of hearsay, expectation, and pretense that mirrors a world where nothing is quite what it claims to be.

✍️ COMMIT, THEN REVEAL · THREE SENTENCES

For each, write your own translation and name the key subjunctive before you open the answer.

1. Hospitēs rogāvērunt num ipse Trīmalchiō vēnisset.

The guests asked whether Trimalchio himself had come. — num introduces a yes/no indirect question; vēnisset = pluperfect subjunctive (secondary sequence after rogāvērunt, action before that verb).

2. Nēmō crēdiderat eum tam dīvitem fuisse.

No one had believed that he had been so wealthy. — fuisse = perfect infinitive of sum in indirect statement (acc. + inf.); crēdiderat = pluperfect indicative main verb.

3. Omnēs hospitēs rīsērunt, quamquam nescīrent cūr rīsissent.

All the guests laughed, although they did not know why they had laughed. — nescīrent = impf. subj. (secondary, same time as rīsērunt); cūr rīsissent = indirect question, pluperfect subj. (action before the not-knowing).

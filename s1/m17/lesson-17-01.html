★ The Capstone — A Roman Figure on Their Own Terms
Lesson · about 35 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
This is the final module of Semester 1 — and instead of one more translation, you become the researcher.
M17 closes the semester with a Roman Figure Study: a 100-point research project on one significant Roman of the late Republic or early Augustan period. There is no separate translation-and-response this module — the project is the work. Today you learn what the project asks of you, how its parts fit together, and how everything you have read since M09 becomes raw material for it. The full brief lives in the Project Brief document; this week's lessons walk you into it.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to explain what the Roman Figure Study is and why it replaces the usual translation-and-response.
2
I will be able to name the four components of the project and how they are submitted.

📚 PART 2 · THE PROJECT 12 min

What the figure study asks of you

Rather than translating an assigned passage, you will build a rounded portrait of one Roman — combining history, reception, and their own Latin. The project has four components, submitted together as a single PDF:

Module 17 — Roman Figure Study
25 ptsEnglish Profile — biography and significance
55 ptsLatin Translation — an authentic primary-source passage, translated
15 ptsEtymology — English descendants of your passage's key words
5 ptsProcess Reflection — how you researched and what you found
Total: 100 pts · Facilitator-graded on all components · Submit as one PDF via Canvas
📅 KEY DATES

The project is due at the end of M17 — consult the Canvas module for the specific deadline. All components (English profile, Latin translation, and etymology) must be submitted together as a single PDF via Canvas.

✅ TRY IT · the reflection
What does the 5-point Process Reflection ask you to describe?

In your own words: why might a capstone project suit the end of a semester better than one more assigned translation? Think it through, then reveal one answer.

A capstone asks you to synthesize — to pull together reading, grammar, history, and research into one self-directed piece. An assigned translation tests a single skill in isolation; the figure study proves you can act as a Latinist, choosing a text, translating it, and situating it in its world.

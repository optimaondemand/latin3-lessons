📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Yesterday you named the verb types. Today you conjugate them.
A deponent takes the same passive endings you already know — -or, -ris, -tur, -mur, -minī, -ntur — and the differences between the 3rd, 3rd-iō, and 4th conjugations are small vowel matters, not new machinery. Today you learn loquor (3rd) in present and imperfect, then the 3rd-iō deponent patior and the 4th conjugation deponent orior. Translate every form actively.

📚 PART 2 · THIRD CONJUGATION DEPONENTS 14 min

The model: loquor

Third conjugation deponents use the passive infinitive -ī (not active -ere) and passive endings throughout. Translate every form actively.

📜 RULE 1 · 3RD CONJUGATION PARADIGM

The stem of loquor is loque-. Passive endings attach directly: -or, -eris, -itur, -imur, -iminī, -untur. The imperfect inserts -bā- before the ending: loquēbar, loquēbāris, loquēbātur…

loquor — Present

loquorI speak
loquerisyou speak
loquiturhe/she speaks
loquimurwe speak
loquiminīyou all speak
loquunturthey speak

loquor — Imperfect

loquēbarI was speaking
loquēbārisyou were speaking
loquēbāturhe/she was speaking
loquēbāmurwe were speaking
loquēbāminīyou all were speaking
loquēbanturthey were speaking
📚 PART 3 · 3RD-IŌ AND 4TH CONJUGATION 10 min

patior vs. orior

3rd-iō deponents (patior, morior) insert an -i- glide in most forms. 4th conjugation deponents (orior) use the long -ī- stem throughout.

📜 RULE 2 · 3RD-IŌ vs. 4TH DEPONENTS

Patitur (3rd-iō): note the -i- between the stem and the ending. Oritur (4th): the -ī- is long and consistent. Both are passive in form, active in meaning: “he suffers,” “he/she rises.”

patior — Present (3rd-iō)

patiorI suffer / allow
paterisyou suffer
patiturhe/she suffers
patimurwe suffer
patiminīyou all suffer
patiunturthey suffer

orior — Present (4th)

oriorI rise / arise
oririsyou rise
oriturhe/she rises
orimurwe rise
oriminīyou all rise
oriunturthey rise

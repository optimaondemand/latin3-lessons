📚 PART 1 · THE SEMI-DEPONENT 12 min

audeō — active now, deponent later

A semi-deponent uses active forms in the present system (present, imperfect, future) but deponent forms — passive ending, active meaning — in the perfect system.

📜 RULE 3 · RECOGNIZING A SEMI-DEPONENT

Dictionary signal: active present infinitive + passive-form perfect. Audeō, audēre, ausus sum. The present audet = “he dares” (active form); the perfect ausus est = “he dared” (passive form, active meaning).

audeō — Present (active forms)

audeōI dare
audēsyou dare
audethe/she dares
audēmuswe dare
audētisyou all dare
audentthey dare

audeō — Perfect (deponent forms)

ausus sumI (have) dared
ausus esyou (have) dared
ausus esthe/she (has) dared
ausī sumuswe (have) dared
ausī estisyou all (have) dared
ausī suntthey (have) dared
⚠️ COMMON CONFUSION

Do NOT read ausus est as passive (“he was dared”). The perfect participle ausus of a semi-deponent carries active meaning. A real passive perfect from a different verb would be e.g., audītus est (“he was heard”).

💡 DON'T MISS · ūTOR TAKES THE ABLATIVE

ūtor does not take an accusative object — it governs the ablative. “I use the plan” is cōnsiliō ūtor, literally something like “I make myself useful with the plan.” Watch for this in tomorrow's reading.

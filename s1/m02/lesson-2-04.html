⏰ PART 1 · READ THE PASSAGE 14 min

Palaestrio Cōnsilium Capit

Read each line aloud first. Find the subject and the verb, decide whether the verb is deponent, semi-deponent, or ordinary, and translate it actively where it should be. The gloss below helps with the harder words.

PALAESTRIO CŌNSILIUM CAPIT · ADAPTED FROM PLAUTUS, MĪLES GLŌRIŌSUS II–III
1.Palaestrio cum Pleusiclē per parietem loquēbātur; haec rēs multās hōrās morābātur.
2.“Philocōmāsia,” inquit, “in hāc domō habitat; dominus eam cēpit et hūc sēcum addūxit.”
3.Pleusiclēs tristis erat et morī volēbat, sed Palaestrio tālia patī nōluerat.
4.“Nōlī,” inquit, “dēspērāre. Mē sequere et cōnsiliō meō ūtere!”
5.Ex hōc dolō novum cōnsilium orīrī poterat: Philocōmāsia soror eius simulābitur.
6.Palaestrio audēbat multa fingere nec umquam mentiendō timēbat.
7.Pleusiclēs cōnsilium audīvit et tandem spem habēre ausus est.
8.“Loquere,” inquit, “et omnia nārrā — libenter tē sequar et dolō tuō ūtar.”
Gloss: parietem — wall/partition (acc.) · hūc — to this place · tālia — such things (acc. pl.) · nōluerat — had not wanted (pluperfect of nōlō) · simulābitur — will be pretended/impersonated (passive, not deponent) · fingere — to fabricate (active infinitive) · mentiendō — by lying (gerund of mentior, itself a deponent) · ūtar — I will use (future of ūtor, deponent)
📚 PART 2 · LITERARY NOTE 8 min
🎭 THE SERVUS CALLIDUS

Palaestrio is the quintessential servus callidus (clever slave) of Roman comedy. Note how line 4 — “Mē sequere et cōnsiliō meō ūtere!” — uses two deponent imperatives. He commands Pleusicles, a free man, using the same authority language a general would use to subordinates. The humor is architectural: the slave is directing the citizen. Watch, too, how line 5's simulābitur is a genuine future passive (“she will be impersonated”) sitting right beside the deponent orīrī — Plautus lets the forms look alike while meaning opposite things.

✍️ PART 4 · TRANSLATE, THEN CHECK 6 min

Write your own translation of each line before you reveal. Attend to the deponent and semi-deponent forms.

1. Palaestrio cum Pleusiclē per parietem loquēbātur.
Palaestrio was speaking with Pleusicles through the wall. loquēbātur: 3rd sg. imperfect deponent → active.
2. “Nōlī dēspērāre. Mē sequere et cōnsiliō meō ūtere!”
“Don't despair! Follow me and use my plan!” sequere: 2nd sg. imperative of sequor (deponent); ūtere: 2nd sg. imperative of ūtor (deponent, +abl.).
3. Pleusiclēs cōnsilium audīvit et tandem spem habēre ausus est.
Pleusicles heard the plan and at last dared to have hope. ausus est: perfect of the semi-deponent audeō — passive form, active meaning “he dared.”

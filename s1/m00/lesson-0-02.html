Lesson 0.02
★ Vox Passīva — When the Subject Receives
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
The passive voice is everywhere in real Latin. Today you make its endings automatic again — and sharpen the distinction that trips up even strong students: agent vs. means.
The passive voice signals that the subject receives the action. You know both systems for all four conjugations. Today we verify that the present-system endings are automatic, review how the perfect system is built from the PPP plus esse, and lock down when Latin uses ā/ab + ablative versus the bare ablative. Every passive sentence you read this year rests on today's material.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to recognize and produce present- and imperfect-passive endings for the 1st conjugation.
2
I will be able to build the perfect and pluperfect passive from PPP + esse.
3
I will be able to distinguish the agent (ā/ab + abl.) from the means (bare ablative).
⏰ PART 1 · RECALL 5 min

Active vs. passive

In the active voice the subject does the action; in the passive the subject receives it. The same event, two viewpoints:

Active

Plautus fābulam scrībit.
Plautus writes a play.

Subject Plautus performs the action.

Passive

Fābula ā Plautō scrībitur.
A play is written by Plautus.

Subject fābula receives the action.
💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · no wrong answer
Without looking ahead: in the passive form amātur, what part of the word signals “is being ___” to you first?
📚 PART 2 · THE TWO SYSTEMS 14 min

Present system: the r-endings

In the present, imperfect, and future the passive is built with a single set of personal endings. Here is amō in the present and imperfect — verify that these are automatic before M01.

Present passiveImperfect passive
1st sg.amor — I am lovedamābar — I was being loved
2nd sg.amārisamābāris
3rd sg.amāturamābātur
1st pl.amāmuramābāmur
2nd pl.amāminīamābāminī
3rd pl.amanturamābantur
💡 THE SIX PASSIVE ENDINGS

Memorize the set once and it works across the whole present system, in every conjugation: -or, -ris, -tur, -mur, -minī, -ntur. Every one carries an r — the sound of the Latin passive.

Perfect system: PPP + esse

The perfect, pluperfect, and future-perfect passive are two words: the perfect passive participle (PPP), which agrees with the subject in gender, number, and case, plus a form of esse.

Perfect passivePluperfect passive
1st sg.amātus sum — I was / have been lovedamātus eram — I had been loved
2nd sg.amātus esamātus erās
3rd sg.amātus estamātus erat
1st pl.amātī sumusamātī erāmus
2nd pl.amātī estisamātī erātis
3rd pl.amātī suntamātī erant

Because the PPP is an adjective, it must agree with the subject: fābula scrīpta est (fem. sg.), but fābulae scrīptae sunt (fem. pl.). Watch that agreement — it often tells you the subject's gender and number at a glance.

📌 PART 3 · AGENT vs. MEANS 6 min

Who did it — and with what?

⚖️ THE DISTINCTION

The agent (the person who performs the action) takes ā/ab + ablative: ā Plautō scrīpta — “written by Plautus.” The means (a thing, not a person) takes the ablative alone: gladiō vulnerātus — “wounded by / with a sword.” This distinction appears in nearly every passive sentence you will read this year.

Mīles ā servō dēcipitur.

The soldier is deceived by the slave. — ā servō = personal agent (ā/ab + abl.).

Mīles dolō dēcipitur.

The soldier is deceived by a trick. — dolō = means/instrument (bare ablative, no preposition).

✅ PART 4 · PUT IT TO WORK 15 min

Five reps across both systems and the agent/means distinction. Keep the charts above in view if you need them; the goal is to make the passive automatic. Wrong answers give a hint — use Reset and try again.

✅ TRY IT · agent or means?
Which sentence expresses means (a thing), not a personal agent?

Commit, then reveal: translate Fābulae ā Plautō scrīptae sunt — and name the tense, the voice, and what ā Plautō is doing. Say your full answer, then reveal.

“The plays were written by Plautus.” Tense: perfect; voice: passive (PPP scrīptae, fem. pl., agreeing with fābulae, + sunt). ā Plautō is the personal agent — ā/ab + ablative, because Plautus is a person.

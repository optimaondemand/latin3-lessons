Lesson 0.01
★ Salvē, Discipule — Welcome to Latin 3 Honors
Lesson · about 35 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Before new grammar begins, take one day to see the whole terrain — where two years of Latin have brought you, and where this course will take you.
Latin 3 Honors is the most grammatically demanding course in the Optima Latin sequence. This module is a single-week review: no new syntax today, just a map. You will name what you already control, preview the road ahead through authentic authors, and take an honest measure of where you stand. Everything you drill this week you learned in Latin 2 — the point is to make it automatic before M01 raises the bar.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to name the major constructions I already control from Latin 2.
2
I will be able to describe the arc of Semester 1 — the new syntax and the three authors ahead.
⏰ PART 1 · WHERE YOU ARE 8 min

Two years of control

You enter this course having completed Latin 2. You can read and produce Latin using the active voice in all tenses, the full passive system, all three participles, ablative absolute constructions, and indirect statement. These are not small things — they represent more syntactic control than most Latin students ever develop.

📜 YOUR LATIN 2 TOOLKIT

Passive voice (present & perfect systems) · the three participles (PAP, PPP, FAP) · the ablative absolute · indirect statement (accusative + infinitive) · agent vs. means. This week reactivates each of these in turn — one per day, Tuesday through Thursday — before you read your first author on Day 4.

💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · no wrong answer
Looking at that list, which construction do you feel least confident about right now?
📚 PART 2 · WHERE YOU ARE GOING 12 min

The road through Semester 1

This semester you will add a formidable set of new tools: deponent verbs, the full subjunctive system (present through pluperfect), purpose and result clauses, indirect question, indirect command, cum clauses, all three types of conditions, the gerund, the gerundive, and the passive periphrastic.

You will read adapted and semi-adapted Latin from Plautus, Petronius, and Cicero — three entirely different registers of the language. Learning to move between the colloquial energy of comedy and the architecture of Ciceronian prose is one of the central goals of this course.

M01
Deponent Verbs I
1st & 2nd conj. deponents · Act I: Pyrgopolīnicēs boasts
M02
Deponent Verbs II
3rd / 3rd-iō / 4th + semi-deponents · Acts II–III: Palaestrio schemes
M03
Subjunctive Mood
Present & imperfect formation · Acts III–IV: deception exposed
M04
Purpose & Result
ut / nē + subjunctive · Plautus finale · Petronius begins
💡 THE PLAUTUS BLOCK

Modules 01–04 use Plautus's comedy Mīles Glōriōsus (“The Braggart Soldier”) as their primary text, introducing one major new grammatical system per module. Cicero's formal prose arrives in M10 — a very different world from the wisecracking slaves of comedy.

What is the purpose of this review module?

In your own words: name the three authors you will read this year and one thing that makes them different from one another. Think it through, then reveal.

Plautus, Petronius, and Cicero. Plautus writes colloquial, energetic comedy full of wordplay and social inversion; Petronius writes prose fiction with a satirical, street-level voice; Cicero writes formal, architected oratory and philosophy. Each is a different register of Latin, and reading across all three is a central goal of the course.

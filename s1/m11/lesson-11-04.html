⏰ PART 1 · THE TEXT 15 min

Cicero on obligation — adapted from De Officiis I.1–10

Read each line aloud. Find the gerundive (-nd-), locate its form of sum, and identify the dative of agent. Work out your own translation before consulting the gloss beneath.

1.Nōbīs semper officium colendum est, sīve in rē pūblicā sīve in prīvātā vīvimus.
2.Carthāgō dēlenda erat — sic Catō clāmāvit; at nōbīs pāx quaerenda est.
3.Mihi ūnum nunc propositum est: ut ea scrībam quae et ūtilia sint et honesta.
4.Cīvibus omnibus rēs pūblica amanda atque tuenda est tamquam parēns commūnis.
5.Ad bellum gerendum legiōnēs erant dūcendae — sed pāx semper petenda est.
6.Quaedam sunt facienda, quaedam nōn facienda — haec est fōns officī.
7.Iūstitia colenda est; iniūria nūllī facienda est, nē dum iniūriam accipis.
8.Tibi haec omnia legenda sunt, Mārce — nōn enim sine labōre sapientia parātur.
Gloss: colendum est — must be cultivated (pass. periphr.; nōbīs = dat. of agent) · sīve…sīve — whether…or · propositum — proposed, set before (perf. pass. part.) · tamquam — just as, as if · parēns commūnis — a common parent · legiōnēs dūcendae — legions to be led (gerundive agrees with legiōnēs) · fōns officī — the source of duty · nē dum — not even when · iniūriam accipis — you receive an injury · Mārce — Marcus (voc.; Cicero's son)
📚 PART 2 · LITERARY NOTE 8 min
⚖️ DE OFFICIIS AND THE GRAMMAR OF OBLIGATION

Cicero wrote De Officiis in 44 BC for his son Marcus, then studying philosophy in Athens. It is his last great work — a father's guide to living honorably, composed in the final year of his life as the Republic collapsed around him.

The passive periphrastic saturates the text because the book is about obligation — what must be done, what must be avoided. The gerundive is not incidental to the style; it is the grammatical embodiment of the ethical argument. When you see -ndus / -nda / -ndum + est / erat, you are reading Cicero's ethics enacted in grammar itself.

📜 A WORD THAT OUTLIVED ROME

Cicero's honestum — “what is honorable” — gives us English honest, and his distinction between the honestum (the moral good) and the ūtile (the useful) shaped Western ethics for two thousand years. When you weigh “the right thing” against “the useful thing,” you are still using Cicero's categories.

✅ TRY IT · the argument
Why does the passive periphrastic dominate De Officiis?

Commit, then reveal. Translate these three lines yourself — write or say a full English version — before opening each answer.

Line 1: Nōbīs semper officium colendum est, sīve in rē pūblicā sīve in prīvātā vīvimus.

“We must always cultivate duty, whether we live in public or in private life.” — colendum est = passive periphrastic; nōbīs = dative of agent, rendered in English as the active subject “we must…”

Line 4: Cīvibus omnibus rēs pūblica amanda atque tuenda est tamquam parēns commūnis.

“The republic must be loved and protected by all citizens like a common parent.” — two gerundives (amanda, tuenda) coordinated with atque; cīvibus = dative of agent for both.

Line 8: Tibi haec omnia legenda sunt, Mārce — nōn enim sine labōre sapientia parātur.

“You must read all these things, Marcus — for wisdom is not acquired without effort.” — legenda sunt = passive periphrastic; tibi = dative of agent; parātur = present passive, “is acquired.”

⏰ PART 1 · WARM-UP 6 min

From gerund to gerundive

You have already met the gerund — the verbal noun that names an activity. It lives only in the singular oblique cases and translates as an English -ing word:

Ars scrībendī est difficilis.

The art of writing is difficult. — scrībendī = gerund, genitive, “of writing.”

Discimus legendō.

We learn by reading. — legendō = gerund, ablative of means.

📚 PART 2 · THE BIG IDEA 12 min

A verbal adjective of obligation

The gerundive (also called the future passive participle) is a verbal adjective. It takes the gerund base and adds full first/second-declension adjective endings — -ndus, -nda, -ndum — and, like any adjective, it agrees with its noun in gender, number, and case. Its core meaning is passive and forward-looking: “to be ___-ed,” “needing to be ___-ed.”

Gerund — verbal noun

scrībendī
of writing (the activity)

Names an action. Singular, oblique cases only. Active in sense.

Gerundive — verbal adjective

liber scrībendus
a book to be written

Describes a noun. Full adjective; agrees in gender/number/case. Passive in sense.

This one form does an astonishing amount of work in Latin. English still carries its fossils: an agenda is literally “things to be done”; memoranda are “things to be remembered”; the name Amanda means “she who must be loved.” When you learn the gerundive, you learn the grammar behind words you already use.

💡 WHERE THIS IS HEADED

Add the verb sum to a gerundive and you get the passive periphrastic — the construction of necessity: Carthāgō dēlenda est, “Carthage must be destroyed.” That is tomorrow's work. Today, just fix the shape -nd- in your eye.

In your own words, before you reveal: how would you explain the difference between the gerund scrībendī (“of writing”) and the gerundive scrībendus (“to be written”) to a Latin 2 student? Say it aloud, then check.

The gerund is a verbal noun: it names the activity itself (“writing”) and is active in sense. The gerundive is a verbal adjective: it attaches to a noun and describes it as something that must be, or is to be, acted upon (“a book to be written”) — passive and forward-looking. Same -nd- stem, opposite grammatical job.

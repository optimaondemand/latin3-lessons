Lesson 11.02
★ Attributive & Periphrastic — The Two Jobs
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Yesterday you learned to spot the gerundive. Today it goes to work — in two ways.
The gerundive does two distinct jobs. As an attributive adjective it simply modifies a noun (“a matter to be done”). Add the verb sum and it becomes the passive periphrastic — the construction of obligation (“it must be done”). Today you learn to form the gerundive from any verb and to read both constructions with confidence. This is the grammatical heart of the module.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…

📚 PART 2 · FORMING THE GERUNDIVE 9 min

Present stem + -nd- + adjective endings

To build a gerundive, take the present stem, add -nd-, then decline it like bonus, -a, -um. (In third and fourth conjugation the linking vowel is -e-: regē + -nd- → regendus.)

VerbStemGerundive (m. nom. sg.)Meaning
amāre (1)amā-amandusto be loved
monēre (2)monē-monendusto be warned
regere (3)reg-regendusto be ruled
audīre (4)audī-audiendusto be heard

Because it is a full adjective, the gerundive has all genders, numbers, and cases: amandus, amanda, amandum; amandī, amandae… It always agrees with the noun it modifies.

📌 PART 3 · THE TWO USES 10 min

Use 1: attributive  |  Use 2: passive periphrastic

Use 1 — Attributive

rēs agenda
a matter to be done

Modifies a noun like any adjective. No form of sum; future-passive sense. Source of English agenda, memoranda, Amanda.

Use 2 — Passive Periphrastic

Carthāgō dēlenda est
Carthage must be destroyed

Gerundive + sum, agreeing with the subject. Expresses obligation / necessity.

The passive periphrastic is simply the attributive gerundive with a form of sum supplied. The subject is the thing that must be acted upon; the tense of sum sets the time:

LatinTranslation
labor faciendus estthe work must be done
labor faciendus eratthe work had to be done
labor faciendus eritthe work will have to be done
💡 WHY “PERIPHRASTIC”?

A periphrasis is a “round-about” expression — here, two words (gerundive + sum) doing the work of a single idea of necessity. English does the same thing with “must be” + past participle: must be destroyed, had to be defended.

📚 PART 1 · MODULE 08 VOCABULARY 12 min

Petronius: The Werewolf Story

Learn these eight. Most are connective adverbs — the small words that stitch a Latin narrative together and control its pace. Watch for all of them in tomorrow's passage.

repente (adv.)
suddenly, all of a sudden
subitō (adv.)
suddenly, unexpectedly
itaque (conj.)
and so, therefore, consequently
praetereā (adv.)
besides, moreover, in addition
dēnique (adv.)
finally, at last; in short
ēbrius, -a, -um
drunk, intoxicated
testāmentum, -ī, n.
will, testament; last will
quippe (adv.)
of course, naturally, to be sure
💡 HONORS NOTE · NARRATIVE ADVERBS

Latin storytelling relies on a small toolkit of time-and-sequence adverbs. Repente and subitō mark a shock; itaque draws a consequence; praetereā adds to the pile; dēnique closes the sequence. Reading them as signposts, not just words, is what lets you follow a fast passage like Petronius without a translation.

📝 PART 3 · COMMIT, THEN REVEAL 7 min

Translate a sentence you will meet tomorrow

Write your own translation first — commit to it — then reveal the model. Name the cum type as part of your answer.

Ille autem, cum lupus esset, ululāre coepit et in silvam fūgit.
But he, since he was a wolf, began to howl and fled into the forest. — Cum + esset (impf. subj.); no tamen, and being a wolf is the reason he howled → causal. Not concessive (no tamen) and not merely historical (it explains, it does not just set the scene).

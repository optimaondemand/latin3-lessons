⏰ PART 1 · READ THE PASSAGE 16 min

Versipellis — adapted from Petronius, Satyrica 61–62

Read each line aloud. For every cum, make the first cut (mood), then name the type. The gloss below carries the harder words — try the line yourself before you lean on it.

  1. Cum Trimalchiō testāmentum suum recitāvisset, Nicerōs fābulam mīrābilissimam narrāre coepit.
  2. Cum iter facerem, inquit, repente comēs meus versipellis factus est.
  3. Cum hoc subitō vīdissem, timor mē cēpit et prope mortuus fuī.
  4. Ille autem, cum lupus esset, ululāre coepit et in silvam fūgit.
  5. Cum timidus essem, tamen domum pervēnī et hospitī meō dīxī quid accidisset.
  6. Hospes, cum haec audīvisset, pālluit — quippe tālia numquam anteā audīverat.
  7. Praetereā dīxit: “Nisi oculīs meīs vīdissem, nōn crēdidissem.”
  8. Dēnique omnēs convīvae, cum haec audīvissent, attonĭtī erant — nēmō rīdēbat.
Gloss: recitāvisset — had read aloud (plupf. subj.) · versipellis — a shape-changer, werewolf (literally “skin-turner”) · ululāre — to howl · pālluit — turned pale (perfect of pallēscō) · quippe — of course, naturally · anteā — before, previously · nisi vīdissem…crēdidissem — unless I had seen…I would not have believed (contrary-to-fact condition, M09 preview) · attonĭtī — thunderstruck, astonished
📚 PART 2 · LITERARY NOTE 6 min
📜 PETRONIUS'S INSET NARRATIVE

The werewolf story (chapters 61–62) is the most famous episode of the Cēna Trimalchiōnis — the “Dinner of Trimalchio.” Petronius embeds it as a first-person tale told by a guest, Niceros: a story within a story within a novel.

Observe how all four cum types appear across these lines — line 1 (historical), line 2 (historical/temporal), line 3 (historical), line 4 (causal), line 5 (concessive + tamen), line 6 (historical), line 8 (historical). The rapid alternation of cum types is a marker of vivid Latin narrative style: each shift re-frames the moment, and the reader who classifies quickly feels the story move.

💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · no wrong answer
Now that you have read it: what makes this passage feel fast and eerie in Latin?

Commit, then reveal — two lines from the passage

Cum timidus essem, tamen domum pervēnī et hospitī meō dīxī quid accidisset.
Although I was afraid, I nevertheless reached home and told my host what had happened. — Tamen in the main clause → concessive cum. Note also quid accidisset = an indirect question in secondary sequence (pluperfect subjunctive).
Dēnique omnēs convīvae, cum haec audīvissent, attonĭtī erant — nēmō rīdēbat.
Finally, all the guests, when they had heard these things, were thunderstruck — no one laughed. — Cum + pluperfect subjunctive, past narrative, no tamen, no causal logic → historical/circumstantial. The clause sets the scene for attonĭtī erant.

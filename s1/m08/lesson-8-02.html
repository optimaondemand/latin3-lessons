📚 PART 1 · THE FOUR RULES 16 min

Cum: when, since, although

Cum with the indicative is a simple temporal clause. Cum with the subjunctive is one of three nuanced types — historical, causal, or concessive — and context tells you which.

RULE 1 · TEMPORAL CUM (INDICATIVE)

Cum + indicative: “when / whenever” — a simple time reference, often with present or future tense. Cum Rōmae erat, multa vīdit — “When he was in Rome, he saw many things.” The indicative signals a simple, neutral time relationship.

RULE 2 · HISTORICAL / CIRCUMSTANTIAL CUM (SUBJUNCTIVE)

Cum + imperfect or pluperfect subjunctive in past narrative: “when” — describes the circumstances surrounding the main action. Often called cum historicum or cum circumstantial. Cum haec dīxisset, abiit — “When he had said these things, he left.” Recognizable by: past narrative context, imperfect or pluperfect subjunctive, no marker of cause or concession.

RULE 3 · CAUSAL CUM (SUBJUNCTIVE)

Cum + subjunctive: “since, because” — the cum clause gives the reason for the main action. Cum esset dīves, multōs amīcōs habēbat — “Since he was rich, he had many friends.” Signal: the cum clause is the cause or explanation; it could often be replaced by quod or quia.

RULE 4 · CONCESSIVE CUM (SUBJUNCTIVE)

Cum + subjunctive + tamen (or similar): “although” — the main clause holds despite the cum clause. Cum ēbrius esset, tamen rēctē loquēbātur — “Although he was drunk, he nevertheless spoke correctly.” Signal: tamen in the main clause is the key marker of concession.

📌 PART 2 · THE DIAGNOSTIC ORDER 8 min

The tamen test — and what comes after it

🛠️ SHORTCUT · THE TAMEN TEST

If tamen appears in the main clause → concessive cum (“although”). If there is no tamen and the cum clause is the cause → causal. If the cum clause just sets the scene in past narrative → historical. Indicative → temporal. Apply the tests in order: tamen test → causal-logic test → narrative test → indicative.

Why an order? Because the tests overlap. A concessive clause is technically in past narrative too — but tamen outranks the narrative reading. Running the tests in sequence stops you from settling for “historical” when a sharper reading is available.

The subjunctive conditions and relatives

ConstructionTrigger / signalMood / tenseTranslation key
Future less vividsī + pres. subj. / pres. subj.pres. subj. both clauses“if…should…, would…”
Contrary-to-fact (present)sī + impf. subj. / impf. subj.impf. subj. both clauses“if…were…, would be…”
Contrary-to-fact (past)sī + plupf. subj. / plupf. subj.plupf. subj. both clauses“if…had been…, would have been…”
Relative of characteristicis / nēmō est quī + subj.pres. / impf. subj.“the sort who… / no one who…”
Relative of purposeverb of sending + quī + subj.pres. / impf. subj.“who would… / in order to…”
💡 THE TENSE IS THE LABEL (CONDITIONS)

For the subjunctive conditions, read the tense of both verbs first and the type names itself: present + present → future-less-vivid (“should…would”); imperfect + imperfect → present contrary-to-fact (“were…would be”); pluperfect + pluperfect → past contrary-to-fact (“had…would have”). Watch for the occasional mixed CTF, where protasis and apodosis differ in tense.

Sī haec dīxisset, errāvisset.

If he had said this, he would have been wrong. — pluperfect + pluperfect subj. → past contrary-to-fact.

Mīsit lēgātōs quī pacem peterent.

He sent envoys to seek peace / who would seek peace. — verb of sending + quī + impf. subj. → relative of purpose.

Nēmō est quī hoc crēdat.

There is no one who believes this / of the sort to believe this. — generalizing antecedent nēmō est + quī + subj. → relative of characteristic.

📌 PART 3 · THE THREAD & THE WORDS 10 min

Sequence of tenses

One rule governs which subjunctive tense a subordinate clause takes. It depends on whether the main verb is primary (present/future/future-perfect) or secondary (any past tense).

Main verbSame-time / afterBefore (completed)
Primary (pres./fut.)present subjunctiveperfect subjunctive
Secondary (past)imperfect subjunctivepluperfect subjunctive

In short: present/perfect subjunctive report action incomplete relative to a primary main verb; imperfect/pluperfect report action after or complete relative to a secondary main verb. This single rule sets the tense in nearly every construction you reviewed this week.

Commit first: how do you tell a relative clause of purpose from one of characteristic? Give the tell, then reveal.

Relative of purpose follows a verb of sending or seeking (mīsit, lēgit) and means “who would… / in order to…” — the clause states an aim. Relative of characteristic follows a generalizing or indefinite antecedent (is est quī, nēmō est quī, sunt quī) and means “the sort who…” — the clause states a quality. Ask: aim or quality?

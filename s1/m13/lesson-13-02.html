📚 PART 2 · FAMILY 1 — THE ut / nē CLAUSES 14 min

Four constructions, four triggers

These four all attach to a main verb and take a subjunctive. What tells them apart is the trigger — the signal word and the kind of main verb it follows.

ConstructionTrigger / signalMood / tenseTranslation key
Purpose clauseut / nē + subj.pres. / impf. subj.“in order to / so that”
Result clauseita / tam / tantus … ut + subj.pres. / impf. subj.“so…that (result)”
Indirect questionmental verb + interrog. word + subj.sequence of tenses“(I ask / know) what / who / whether…”
Indirect commandordering verb + ut / nē + subj.pres. / impf. subj.“(he ordered) that…”
💡 PURPOSE vs. RESULT — THE HONORS DISTINCTION

Both use ut + subjunctive. Two tells separate them. (1) Signposts: result clauses are cued by a word of degree earlier in the sentence — ita, tam, tantus, sīc, adeō. Purpose clauses are not. (2) Negative: purpose negates with nē; result negates with ut nōn. If you see ut…nōn, it is result every time.

Vēnit ut senātum moveēret.

He came in order to move the senate. — purpose: ut + impf. subj., no word of degree.

Tam ācriter dīxit ut omnēs mōverēntur.

He spoke so fiercely that all were moved. — result: tam signposts it; ut + impf. subj. reports the outcome.

Imperāvit lēgātīs ut dīscēderent.

He ordered the officers to depart. — indirect command: ordering verb imperāvit + ut + subj.

Rogāvit quid Cicero dīxisset.

He asked what Cicero had said. — indirect question: mental verb + interrogative quid + subj. (plupf. by sequence).

📌 PART 3 · FAMILY 2 — THE FOUR FACES OF cum 10 min

One word, four jobs

Cum is the great chameleon of the subjunctive system. Mood, tense, and small signal words decide which of the four it is.

ConstructionTrigger / signalMood / tenseTranslation key
Cum temporalcum + indicativeindicative“when” (factual)
Cum historical / circumstantialcum + impf./plupf. subj.impf. / plupf. subj.“when” (in narrative)
Cum causalcum + subj. (= reason)impf. / plupf. subj.“since, because”
Cum concessivecum + subj. + tamenimpf. / plupf. subj.“although”

Rule of thumb: indicative → plain temporal “when.” Subjunctive in past narrative → historical/circumstantial by default. Watch for tamen in the main clause (→ concessive “although”) or a clear cause-and-effect logic (→ causal “since”).

Commit first: how do you tell a purpose clause from a result clause when both use ut + subjunctive? Name both tells, then reveal.

(1) Signpost word: a result clause is set up by a degree word earlier — ita, tam, tantus, sīc, adeō; purpose has none. (2) Negative: purpose negates with nē, result with ut nōn. See ut…nōn or a degree word → result. See a bare ut/nē aimed at a goal → purpose.

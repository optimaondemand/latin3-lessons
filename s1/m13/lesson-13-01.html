⏰ PART 1 · WARM-UP 6 min

Indicative vs. subjunctive — the feel of it

The indicative states facts: this happened, this is true. The subjunctive is the mood of everything that stands one step off from bare fact — purpose, result, possibility, the hypothetical, the reported, the characteristic. Two years of Latin have handed you more than a dozen ways it does this.

Cicero venit.

Cicero came. — plain indicative statement of fact.

Cicero venit ut rem pūblicam servāret.

Cicero came in order to save the republic. — servāret is subjunctive: it reports a purpose, not a fact accomplished.

📚 PART 2 · THE BIG PICTURE 14 min

Three families under one roof

Every subjunctive construction you learned in S1 fits into one of three families. Learn to sort a clause into its family first — the exact label follows quickly once you know which room you are standing in.

Family 1 — ut / nē clauses

Purpose, result, indirect command, and indirect question all hang off a main verb and are introduced by a signal word: ut, nē, or an interrogative. What the action is aimed at, or what it produces.

Family 2 — cum clauses

One little word, four jobs: temporal (indicative), historical/circumstantial, causal, and concessive. The mood and context decide which. The circumstances around the main action.

Family 3 — conditions & relatives

Future-less-vivid and contrary-to-fact conditions, plus the relative clauses of characteristic and of purpose. The hypothetical and the qualifying.

The thread — sequence of tenses

Running through all three families: a primary main verb takes present/perfect subjunctive; a secondary main verb takes imperfect/pluperfect. Master this and half the errors disappear.

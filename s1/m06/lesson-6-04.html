Lesson 6.04
★ Hospitēs Loquuntur — Whispers at Trimalchio's Table
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Rules plus vocabulary equals reading. Today the pieces click together — in a real Latin author.
You read an adapted passage from Petronius' Satyrica (chapters 37–46), where the guests at the freedman Trimalchio's absurd dinner party can only whisper their questions. Almost every line is an indirect question — and that is exactly the point. No new grammar today: today you use what you built, and you see why an author would choose this construction.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to read and translate Petronius' indirect questions, applying sequence of tenses.
2
I will be able to explain how Petronius uses indirect speech as satire.
⏰ PART 1 · READ THE PASSAGE 15 min

Hospitēs Loquuntur — Adapted from Petronius, Satyrica 37–46

Read each line aloud. Find the verb of mental activity, then the interrogative word, then the subjunctive — and use sequence of tenses to decide whether the embedded action is at the same time as, or before, the main verb. Use the gloss below for the hard words.

1.Convīvae inter sē susurrābant et rogābant quid sibi vellent omnia quae vīdissent. 2.Nēmō sciēbat quis esset Trīmalchiō aut unde tam magna cōpia argentī eī fuisset. 3.Quīdam quaerēbant cūr tam superbus esset quī modo servus fuisset. 4.Alius dīxit sē nescīre num Trīmalchiō plūs aurī an argentī possidēret. 5.Scīlicet nēmō dubitābat quīn dīves esset — sed quam dīves, nēmō dīcere poterat. 6.Trīmalchiō ipse nārrāvit quanta esset rēs sua et quantum in annō lucrārētur. 7.Convīvae mīrātī sunt quantum nimius apparātus sibi cōnstitisset. 8.Tandem Trīmalchiō rogāvit num omnēs bene cēnāssent — nēmō respondēre ausus est.
🔖 GLOSS

susurrābant — were whispering · quid sibi vellent — what they meant (lit. “what they wanted for themselves”) · modo — just recently · num…an — whether…or (double indirect question) · quīn — but that (after negative dubitō) · rēs — property, estate · lucrārētur — he was profiting (deponent impf. subj. of lucror) · cōnstitisset — had cost (pluperf. subj. of cōnstō + dat.) · cēnāssent — syncopated = cēnāvissent

📚 PART 2 · LITERARY NOTE 8 min
🍷 TRIMALCHIO'S DINNER · SATYRICA, THE CĒNA

Petronius (1st c. AD, a courtier of Nero) wrote the Satyrica, a satirical novel whose most famous episode is the Cēna Trīmalchiōnis — the “Dinner of Trimalchio.” Trimalchio is a freedman (a former slave, quī modo servus fuisset) who has grown grotesquely rich and now stages a dinner of vulgar excess to display his wealth. The other guests, embarrassed and fascinated, whisper their questions among themselves.

This is a portrait of the newly rich in Neronian Rome — a world where money has outrun taste, and where nobody dares say plainly what everybody is thinking.

💭 WHISPERING AS SATIRE

Petronius renders the guests' table-talk almost entirely in indirect speech — not direct quotation but whispered, embedded questions. The effect is doubly satirical: the guests cannot speak directly (social anxiety), and their indirect questions reveal exactly what Petronius wants us to see — their confusion, envy, and fascinated incomprehension of Trimalchio's world. Line 8's silence after Trimalchio's yes/no question is the punchline: nobody dares even answer a simple num.

📝 PART 4 · COMMIT, THEN REVEAL 5 min

Translate three lines yourself

Write your own translation of each line before you reveal. Then check both the meaning and the sequence-of-tenses reasoning.

1. Nēmō sciēbat quis esset Trīmalchiō aut unde tam magna cōpia argentī eī fuisset.
No one knew who Trimalchio was, or from where such a great abundance of silver had come to him. — Two parallel indirect questions after secondary sciēbat: quis esset (same time, impf. subj.) + unde…fuisset (before, plupf. subj.).
2. Alius dīxit sē nescīre num Trīmalchiō plūs aurī an argentī possidēret.
Another said that he did not know whether Trimalchio possessed more gold or silver. — Outer clause: indirect statement (sē nescīre). Inner clause: indirect question (num…an, a double question), impf. subj. after secondary dīxit.
3. Scīlicet nēmō dubitābat quīn dīves esset — sed quam dīves, nēmō dīcere poterat.
Obviously no one doubted that he was rich — but how rich, no one could say. — dubitābat quīn + subj. = “doubted but that” (Rule 3). The second clause is an indirect question: quam + subj. in secondary sequence.

Lesson 6.01
★ The Embedded Question — From Quis est? to Rogāvit quis esset
Lesson · about 35 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
This module you learn to tuck a question inside another sentence — and to hear the subjunctive that marks it.
A direct question asks outright: Quis est? — “Who is he?” An indirect question embeds that same question inside a larger clause, after a verb of asking, knowing, perceiving, or doubting: Rogāvit quis esset — “He asked who he was.” In the embedded clause the verb goes into the subjunctive, and sequence of tenses applies. Today there is no paradigm to drill — just one big idea to recognize on sight. This week it leads us to Petronius' dinner party, where the guests can only whisper their questions.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to distinguish a direct question from an indirect question.
2
I will be able to identify the two signals of an indirect question: an interrogative word and a subjunctive verb after a verb of mental activity.
⏰ PART 1 · WARM-UP 5 min

A question inside a sentence

In English you do this constantly. “Who is he?” is a direct question. But say “I wonder who he is” and the question has become a clause inside a bigger sentence — an indirect question. Latin marks that shift by putting the embedded verb into the subjunctive.

💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · no wrong answer
When you say “I don't know where he went,” what has happened to the question “Where did he go?”
📚 PART 2 · THE BIG IDEA 12 min

Direct vs. indirect

A direct question uses the indicative and stands on its own. An indirect question is embedded after a verb of mental activity — rogō, sciō, nesciō, quaerō, videō, audiō, dubitō, mīror — and its verb goes into the subjunctive. Watch the same three questions cross over:

Direct Question

Quis est?
Who is he?

Cūr tacēs?
Why are you silent?

Quantum habet?
How much does he have?

Indirect Question

Rogāvit quis esset.
He asked who he was.

Sciō cūr taceās.
I know why you are silent.

Quaesīvit quantum habēret.
He asked how much he had.
💡 TWO SIGNALS, EVERY TIME

Every indirect question shows the same two marks: (1) an interrogative word (quis, quid, cūr, ubi, quantus…) that carries the original question, and (2) a subjunctive verb where a direct question would have the indicative (est → esset, tacēs → taceās). Tomorrow you'll learn exactly which subjunctive tense to pick; today just learn to see both marks.

✅ TRY IT · the second signal
Besides an interrogative word, an indirect question always puts its embedded verb into the …

Commit first, then check: the sentence Rogāvit quid facerent is an indirect question. What does it mean, and which word is the subjunctive? Say your answer aloud, then reveal.

“He asked what they were doing.” Rogāvit (a verb of asking) triggers the indirect question; quid is the interrogative word; facerent is the imperfect subjunctive. Both signals are present — interrogative word + subjunctive.

📚 PART 2 · THE FORMULA & THE WORDS 12 min

Rule 1 — the formula

📌 RULE 1 · FORMULA

Verb of mental activity (rogō, sciō, nesciō, quaerō, videō, audiō, dubitō, mīror…) + interrogative word (quis, quid, quālis, quantus, quam, ubi, quandō, cūr, num, utrum…an, -ne) + subjunctive verb. Sequence of tenses applies: a primary main verb takes present or perfect subjunctive; a secondary (past) main verb takes imperfect or pluperfect subjunctive.

Rule 2 — the interrogative words

📌 RULE 2 · INTERROGATIVE WORDS

quis / quid (who / what) · quālis (what kind) · quantus (how great) · quam (how [adv.]) · ubi (where) · quandō (when) · cūr (why) · num (whether) · utrum…an (whether…or) · -ne enclitic (yes/no).

💡 ONE WARNING ABOUT num

In a direct question, num expects the answer “no.” But in an indirect question, num simply means “whether” and carries no built-in negative. Nesciō num vēnerit = “I do not know whether he came,” not “…that he surely did not come.”

📚 PART 3 · SEQUENCE OF TENSES 10 min

Choosing the right subjunctive

Two questions decide the tense: (1) Is the main verb primary (present/future) or secondary (past)? (2) Is the embedded action at the same time as the main verb, or before it?

SequenceSame-time subordinateBefore (prior) subordinate
Primary (present/future main)present subjunctiveperfect subjunctive
Secondary (past main)imperfect subjunctivepluperfect subjunctive
Rogat quid faciās.

He asks what you are doing. — primary main (rogat) + same time → present subj. faciās.

Rogāvit quid fēcissēs.

He asked what you had done. — secondary main (rogāvit) + prior action → pluperfect subj. fēcissēs.

Rule 3 — dubitō quīn

📌 RULE 3 · DUBITŌ QUĪN

After a negative or implied-negative dubitō, Latin uses quīn + subjunctive (not num): Nēmō dubitābat quīn dīves esset — “No one doubted that he was rich.” Here quīn = “but that” and carries an affirmative embedded meaning. Quīn + subjunctive also follows nōn dubitō, haud dubitō, and verbs of preventing.

🧩 DIAGNOSTIC · INDIRECT QUESTION VS. PURPOSE / RESULT

Indirect question: interrogative word + subjunctive, after a mental-activity verb — no ut. Purpose clause: ut / nē + subjunctive, after a verb of motion or action. Result clause: ut + subjunctive, after ita, tam, tantus. Relative clause of characteristic: quī + subjunctive. When in doubt, identify the introducing word first.

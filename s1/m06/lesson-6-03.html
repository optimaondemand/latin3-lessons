💡 VERBS THAT TRIGGER INDIRECT QUESTIONS

Four of these words are verbs of mental activity — rogō, nesciō, quaerō, dubitō — so they are exactly the verbs that set up an indirect question. Learn them as a set: whenever one appears, expect an interrogative word and a subjunctive to follow.

📌 PART 2 · EMBED A QUESTION 10 min

From direct to indirect, step by step

To embed a direct question: (1) keep the interrogative word, (2) shift the indicative verb into the subjunctive, (3) pick the tense by sequence of tenses, driven by the new introducing verb.

Direct

Quid facit?
What is he doing?

Indirect (after Rogō)

Rogō quid faciat.
I ask what he is doing.
primary main → present subj.
Ubi est? → Nesciēbam ubi esset.

Where is he? → I did not know where he was. — secondary main (nesciēbam) + same time → imperfect subj.

Commit first, then check: embed the direct question Cūr tacet? (“Why is he silent?”) after the past-tense verb Quaesīvit (“He asked…”). Write your version, then reveal.

Quaesīvit cūr tacēret. — “He asked why he was silent.” Keep cūr; the past main verb quaesīvit gives secondary sequence, and the silence is contemporaneous → imperfect subjunctive tacēret.

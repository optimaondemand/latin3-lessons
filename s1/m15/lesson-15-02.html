📚 PART 2 · THREE DEVICES 14 min

Anaphora, apostrophe, and the rhetorical question

Anaphora is the deliberate repetition of the same word at the start of successive clauses. Cicero uses it as a drumbeat — each repetition raises the pressure by one notch.

Nihilō tē nocturnum praesidium Palātī, nihil urbis vigiliae, nihil timor populī, nihil concursus bonōrum omnium movēbat?

Fourfold nihil — five subjects piled up, one verb (movēbat) at the very end. Each nihil is another hammer stroke.

Abiit, excessit, ēvāsit, ērūpit.

Four verbs in a row — “he went away, departed, escaped, burst out” — a triumphant staccato marking Catiline's flight.

Apostrophe is direct address — the speaker turns and speaks to someone (or something), often abruptly. Cicero addresses Catiline by name in the Senate, and even addresses the age itself:

Ō tempora, ō mōrēs!

“O the times! O the customs!” — apostrophe to abstractions, a cry of moral outrage.

A rhetorical question is a question asked not for information but for effect — the answer is already obvious, and the question forces the audience to supply it. Which answer the audience is being pushed toward depends on the particle Cicero attaches. That is Part 3.

📌 PART 3 · THE QUESTION PARTICLES & THE CTF WEAPON 10 min

Which answer does the question expect?

ParticleExpects the answerForce
-ne (enclitic)neutral / opena genuine yes/no question — Nihilne tē…movēbat?
nōnne“yes”“surely…?” — pushes the audience to agree
num“no”“surely not…?” — the answer is meant to be denied

Now the sharpest tool. The past contrary-to-fact condition imagines something that did not happen and states its unreal result. Both clauses take the pluperfect subjunctive:

Quod sī hoc…tempore patriā exire iussus essēs, nōnne omnia…monumenta dēlēta essent?

iussus essēs = pluperf. subj. passive (protasis, the “if” clause). dēlēta essent = pluperf. subj. passive (apodosis, the result). Both unreal — and nōnne forces a “yes.”

⚖️ GRAMMAR AS WEAPON

Notice what Cicero achieves. By phrasing Catiline's overdue punishment as an unreal past condition, he simultaneously (1) reminds everyone it should have happened, (2) implies his own restraint in not doing it, and (3) via nōnne makes the Senate mentally agree. The construction is textbook; the effect is devastating.

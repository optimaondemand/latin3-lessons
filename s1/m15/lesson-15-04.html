⏰ PART 1 · READING I 13 min

The opening — In Catilinam I.1–4, lightly adapted

Read the whole passage aloud once for rhythm, then again slowly with the gloss. Cicero addresses Catiline directly before the Senate.

IN CATILINAM I — CICERO ADDRESSES CATILINE BEFORE THE SENATE (ADAPTED)
1.Quō ūsque tandem abūtēre, Catilīna, patientiā nostrā? Quam diū etiam furor iste tuus nos ēlūdet? 2.Nihilō tē nocturnum praesidium Palātī, nihil urbis vigiliae, nihil timor populī, nihil concursus bonōrum omnium movēbat? 3.Patēre tua cōnsilia nōn sentīs? Cōnstrictam iam omnium hōrum scientiā tenērī coniūrātiōnem tuam nōn vidēs? 4.Ō tempora, ō mōrēs! Senātus haec intellegit, cōnsul videt — hic tamen vīvit. 5.Vīvit? Imo vērō etiam in senātum venit, fit pūblicī cōnsilī particeps, notat et dēsignat oculīs ad caedem ūnum quemque nostrum. 6.Nōs autem, fortēs virī, satis facere reī pūblicae vidēmur sī istīus furōrem ac tēla vītāmus. 7.Ad mortem tē, Catilīna, dūcī iussū cōnsulis iam prīdem oportēbat — in tē conferrī pestem quam tū in nōs machīnāris. 8.Quod sī hoc, Catilīna, tempore patriā exire iussus essēs, nōnne omnia istīus audāciae monumenta dēlēta essent?
Gloss: quō ūsque tandem — how far, finally? · abūtēre — you will abuse (future 2nd sing. of abūtor, deponent) · ēlūdet — will mock · nocturnum praesidium Palātī — nightly guard of the Palatine · concursus bonōrum — gathering of all good citizens · cōnstrictam tenērī — to be held bound · imo vērō — no, on the contrary · particeps — partaker (takes gen.: pūblicī cōnsilī) · notat et dēsignat oculīs — marks and singles out with his eyes · iussū cōnsulis — by the consul's order · pestem — plague, destruction · machīnāris — you are plotting (deponent) · iussus essēs / dēlēta essent = past CTF condition
🏛️ HISTORICAL CONTEXT — CATILINARIAN CRISIS, 63 BC

In 63 BC, Cicero as consul uncovered Catiline's plot to overthrow the government. On November 8 he delivered this speech before the Senate with Catiline himself present in the chamber. The speech is partly genuine crisis rhetoric, partly a performance designed to shame Catiline into voluntary exile — which it achieved. Catiline left Rome that night. He died in battle against Roman forces in Etruria in early 62 BC.

📚 PART 2 · READING II 9 min

After Catiline leaves — In Catilinam II.1–3, lightly adapted

The next day, Catiline gone, Cicero speaks to the Roman people. Hear the tempo shift from confrontation to triumph.

IN CATILINAM II — CICERO ADDRESSES THE ROMAN PEOPLE (ADAPTED)
9.Abiit, excessit, ēvāsit, ērūpit — Catilīna discessit! 10.Quam rem pūblicam habēmus? In quā urbe vīvimus? Quid sentīmus? 11.At enim, Rōmānī, id quod maximē volō, vōs quoque id scīre dēbētis: Catilīnam vīvum expulsī sumus. 12.Nōlīte arbitrārī mē mala improbōrum extimēscere — ego enim tūtus sum sī vōs tūtī estis.
Gloss: abiit, excessit, ēvāsit, ērūpit — went away, departed, escaped, burst out (4-verb anaphora for dramatic triumph) · quod maximē volō — which I most want (relative clause: “the thing which…”) · vīvum — alive, predicative accusative (we expelled him while he was still alive — emphasis on the mercy Cicero gave Catiline) · nōlīte arbitrārī — do not suppose (neg. imperative + inf.) · improbōrum — of the wicked · extimēscere — to greatly fear

Commit then reveal — translate line 2: Nihilō tē nocturnum praesidium Palātī, nihil urbis vigiliae, nihil timor populī, nihil concursus bonōrum omnium movēbat? Write your version, then check.

“Did the nightly guard of the Palatine move you not at all — not the city's night-watches, not the fear of the people, not the gathering of all the good citizens?” nihilō…nihil…nihil…nihil = quadruple anaphora. The -ne on the first element signals the yes/no question. Movēbat = imperfect indicative — five subjects, one verb at the very end.

One more, commit then reveal — translate line 8: Quod sī hoc, Catilīna, tempore patriā exire iussus essēs, nōnne omnia istīus audāciae monumenta dēlēta essent? Then check.

“But if at this moment, Catiline, you had been ordered to leave your fatherland, would not all the monuments of that man's audacity have been destroyed?” iussus essēs = past CTF protasis (pluperf. subj. passive). dēlēta essent = past CTF apodosis (pluperf. subj. passive). Nōnne expects “yes” — rhetorical.

📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
A direct command shouts Venī! An indirect command reports it — and Latin has a precise machinery for doing so.
This week's construction is the indirect command: how Latin embeds an order inside a larger sentence, using ut or nē plus the subjunctive. It is the third and last of your three great noun-clause constructions — you already own indirect statement (acc. + infinitive) and indirect question (interrogative + subjunctive). Today is orientation: no full paradigm to drill, just the idea, the shape of the construction, and the eight vocabulary words that will carry the module. We anchor everything in a single scene — Trimalchio's outrageous dinner party in Petronius's Satyrica, a feast run entirely by commands.

⏰ PART 1 · WARM-UP 5 min

Direct vs. reported

In English the difference is intuitive. A direct command quotes the order exactly: “Come!” An indirect (reported) command folds it into a sentence: She ordered them to come / She ordered that they come. Latin does the same — but where English reaches for an infinitive (“to come”), Latin usually reaches for ut + subjunctive.

Direct — imperative

Venī!
“Come!”

The order stands alone, in the imperative mood.

Indirect — reported

Imperāvit ut venīrent.
“He ordered that they come.”

The order is embedded after a verb of commanding, with ut + subjunctive.

💡 HOW IT DIFFERS FROM ITS COUSINS

Unlike indirect question, there is no interrogative word (cūr, quid, num…). Unlike indirect statement, there is no accusative + infinitive. The tell-tale mark of the indirect command is the plain connector ut or nē leading a subjunctive verb.

📝 PART 3 · THE MODULE'S EIGHT WORDS 8 min

Vocabulary — commands at the table

Five of these are verbs of commanding or urging — the very verbs that trigger indirect command. Note carefully which take the dative of the person commanded and which take the accusative; you will need this tomorrow.

imperō, -āre (+ dat.)
to command, order (someone)
ōrō, -āre
to beg, pray, implore
moneō, -ēre
to warn, advise, remind
prohibeō, -ēre
to prevent, forbid, restrain
persuādeō, -ēre (+ dat.)
to persuade (someone)
convīvium, -ī, n.
dinner party, banquet, feast
splendor, -ōris, m.
brilliance, splendor, magnificence
modus, -ī, m.
measure, limit, manner, way
📜 DERIVATIVES TO ANCHOR THEM

imperō → imperative, empire, imperious · persuādeō → persuade, suasion, dissuade · splendor → splendid, resplendent · modus → mode, moderate, modest, modify.

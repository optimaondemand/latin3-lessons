⏰ PART 1 · THE PASSAGE 15 min

Petronius finale & Cicero's world — eight lines

Read each line aloud. Find the protasis and apodosis, read both verbs, and classify the condition before you consult the gloss.

📜 PETRONIUS, SATYRICON (ADAPTED) & CICERO, AD ATTICUM I.1 (ADAPTED)
1.Nīsi ē convīviō fūgissēmus, numquam lībertātem adeptī essēmus — ita tenāx erat Trīmalchiō.
2.Sī quis haec lēgisset nec rīsisset, nōn intellēxisset quid Petrōnius scrīpsisset.
3.[Cicerō ad Atticum:] Sī quid habes novī, certiōrem mē facies — ego tē de meis rebus docēbō.
4.Sī Rōmae mansissem, plūra agere potuissem — at exsilium multa mihi ademit.
5.Nisi amīcōs haberem, vix rem pūblicam servāre possem — tanta est vis amīcitiae.
6.Sī quis mē roget quid in bonīs sit, respondēbō: amīcitia, sapientia, virtūs.
7.Sī hoc scīrēs, longe aliter sentīrēs dē rēbus quae nunc tē vexant.
8.At sī aliter fēcissēs, et tū et rēs pūblica multo meliōrēs esse potuissētis.
GLOSS

tenāx — clinging, tenacious, hard to escape  ·  adeptī essēmus — we would have obtained (plupf. subj., deponent adipīscor)  ·  certiōrem mē facies — you will inform me (literally “you will make me more certain”)  ·  exsilium — exile  ·  ademit — took away (perfect of adimō)  ·  vis amīcitiae — the power of friendship  ·  sentīrēs — you would feel / think (impf. subj., future less vivid)  ·  vexant — trouble, harass

📚 PART 2 · THE LITERARY PIVOT 8 min
📚 LITERARY PIVOT · PETRONIUS TO CICERO

M09 bridges the Petronius block (M05–M09) and opens the Cicero block (M10–M17). Lines 1–2 close out the Cēna with conditions that sum up its satirical logic. Lines 3–8 introduce Ciceronian register: shorter, more balanced sentences; philosophical weight; personal and political stakes. Notice immediately that Cicero's conditions tend toward the personal and abstract, while Petronius's are embedded in narrative chaos.

✍️ PART 4 · COMMIT, THEN REVEAL 10 min

Translate three, then check

Write a full translation of each in your notebook before revealing. Note the condition type as you go.

1. Nisi ē convīviō fūgissēmus, numquam lībertātem adeptī essēmus.
“If we had not fled from the dinner party, we would never have obtained our freedom.” Nisi + plupf. subj. / plupf. subj. → past contrary-to-fact. Adeptī essēmus is the pluperfect subjunctive of the deponent adipīscor.
2. Nisi amīcōs haberem, vix rem pūblicam servāre possem.
“If I did not have friends, I could hardly preserve the republic.” Nisi + impf. subj. / impf. subj. → present contrary-to-fact. The situation is real (Cicero does have friends), but he imagines the alternative.
3. Sī quis mē roget quid in bonīs sit, respondēbō: amīcitia, sapientia, virtūs.
“If anyone should ask me what is among the good things, I will answer: friendship, wisdom, virtue.” Sī + present subj. (roget) / future indicative (respondēbō) — a mixed condition: a future-less-vivid protasis with a simple apodosis. Cicero slides between types fluidly.

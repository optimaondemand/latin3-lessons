📚 PART 2 · PURPOSE CLAUSES 12 min

Formula one: the purpose clause

A purpose clause answers “why?” or “in order to do what?” It is introduced by ut (positive) or nē (negative) and uses the subjunctive — present after a primary main verb, imperfect after a secondary (past) main verb.

⚖️ RULE 1 · PURPOSE-CLAUSE FORMULA

Main verb + ut / nē + present or imperfect subjunctive = purpose clause. The negative connector is always nē, never ut nōn. English translations: “in order to,” “to,” “so that,” or “lest.”

LatinTranslationClause type
Venit ut Philocōmasiam videat.He comes in order to see Philocomasium.Primary: pres. subj.
Vēnit ut Philocōmasiam vidēret.He came in order to see Philocomasium.Secondary: impf. subj.
Fugit nē mīles eum caperet.He fled so that the soldier would not capture him / lest the soldier capture him.Negative purpose
💡 SEQUENCE OF TENSES

A primary main verb (present, future, or perfect-with-have) takes the present subjunctive. A secondary main verb (imperfect, perfect-as-simple-past, pluperfect) takes the imperfect subjunctive. This same sequence governs result clauses too.

📚 PART 3 · RESULT CLAUSES 10 min

Formula two: the result clause

A result clause expresses a consequence — what actually happened. It is also introduced by ut (positive) or ut nōn (negative), but it is signaled in the main clause by a word of intensity or quantity: ita, tam, sīc, tantus, tot, tālis.

⚖️ RULE 2 · RESULT-CLAUSE FORMULA

ita / tam / tantus / tālis + main verb + ut + subjunctive = result clause. For negative result: ut nōn (not nē). English: “so… that,” “with the result that,” “such that.”

LatinTranslationClause type
Mīles ita glōriōsus erat ut omnēs eum vidērent.The soldier was so boastful that everyone watched him.Result: ita + ut
Trīmalchiō tanta cōpiā ūsus est ut nēmō crēderet.Trimalchio used such great abundance that no one believed it.Result: tantus + ut
Tam stultus erat ut dolum nōn intellegeret.He was so stupid that he did not understand the trick.Negative result: ut nōn
✅ PART 4 · PIN DOWN THE DISTINCTION 14 min
⚠️ PURPOSE vs. RESULT — THE CRITICAL DISTINCTION

Purpose: no intensity word in the main clause; nē for negative; answers “why?” Result: ita / tam / tantus / tālis / tot in the main clause; ut nōn for negative; answers “what happened as a result?” Both use ut for the positive form — the main-clause signal is the only reliable guide.

⏰ PART 1 · SIDE BY SIDE 8 min

Purpose vs. result at a glance

Purpose clause

Signal: no ita / tam / tālis in main clause
Connector: ut (pos.) / nē (neg.)
Question: why? for what purpose?

Currit ut perveniat.
“He runs in order to arrive.”

Result clause

Signal: ita / tam / tantus / tālis / tot in main clause
Connector: ut (pos.) / ut nōn (neg.)
Question: what happened as a result?

Ita celeriter currit ut perveniat.
“He runs so fast that he arrives.”

Notice how one small word — ita — flips the identical ut clause from purpose to result. Train your eye to sweep the main clause for that signal before you decide.

🍷 PREVIEW · PETRONIUS VOCABULARY

Convīvium, dīves, lībertus, ostentō are the cornerstone words of the Cēna Trīmalchiōnis. Trimalchio is a freed slave who became fabulously wealthy and hosts a lavish, excessive dinner party — the target of Petronius's satire. These four words describe both the host and the event.

Two more, out loud: Tacuit nē eum cerneret and Intrāvit ut glōriam ostentāret — classify each and say how you know. Then reveal.

Both are purpose clauses. Neither main clause has an intensity word. Tacuit nē eum cerneret = “he fell silent so that he would not notice him” (negative purpose, hence nē). Intrāvit ut glōriam ostentāret = “he entered in order to show off his glory.”

📚 PART 2 · THE SIX FAMILIES 18 min

What fills these passages

ConstructionSignalHow to read it
Relative clause of characteristicindefinite antecedent + subjunctive (sunt quī…, nēmō est quī…)“the sort of person who…” — not a specific person but a type
Result clausetantus/tam/ita…ut + subjunctive“so great…that…” — the ut marks the outcome
Conditional (contrary-to-fact)sī + subjunctive in both halvespluperf. = past CTF (“had … would have”); imperf. = present CTF (“were … would”); halves can mix
Indirect statementhead verb of saying/thinking + accusative + infinitivetense of infinitive is relative to the main verb; future infinitive = “was going to”
Indirect questionquestion word + subjunctive (uter…esset, quam multī…fuissent)read by sequence of tenses (see Part 3)
Passive periphrasticgerundive (-ndus, -a, -um) + form of esseobligation: “must be / ought to be ___ed”

Ordinary relative

radīce quae virtūs vocātur
“the root which is called virtue” — indicative (vocātur): plain description of a real thing.

Relative of characteristic

nēmō est quī…beātus esse possit
“there is no one of the kind who could be happy” — subjunctive (possit): defines a type, not an individual.
⚖️ DEPONENTS — PASSIVE FORM, ACTIVE MEANING

Watch for recordor, recordārī (“to recall”) and mōror, mōrārī (“to delay/tarry,” here of character, “to be of a certain character”). They wear passive endings but translate actively: recordābātur = “he was recalling,” not “he was being recalled.” Cicero uses them constantly.

📌 PART 3 · SEQUENCE OF TENSES 8 min

Primary vs. secondary sequence

In indirect questions and other subjunctive clauses, the tense of the subjunctive is chosen by the tense of the main verb. This is sequence of tenses, and it tells you the time relationship at a glance.

Main verbSequenceSubjunctive tenses used
present / future / perfect-with-haveprimarypresent (same/after), perfect (before)
imperfect / perfect-as-past / pluperfectsecondaryimperfect (same/after), pluperfect (before)
…quī recēperit eum…

Primary sequence: recēperit = perfect subjunctive = action before a present-frame main verb (“whoever has received”).

Catō recordābātur quam multa studia…fuissent.

Secondary sequence: main verb recordābātur (imperfect) → fuissent = pluperfect subjunctive in the indirect question (“how many pursuits there had been”).

✅ TRY IT · sequence
In recordābātur…quam multī amīcī…fuissent, why is fuissent pluperfect subjunctive?

Commit first: in the mixed condition sī quis…vīdisset, nōn intellegeret…, name the type of condition and say why the two halves differ in tense. Then reveal.

A mixed contrary-to-fact condition. The protasis sī…vīdisset is pluperfect subjunctive → past contrary-to-fact (“if anyone had seen”). The apodosis nōn intellegeret is imperfect subjunctive → present contrary-to-fact (“he would not understand”). Cicero mixes them to say: the past encounter would have left a lasting present impossibility of telling host from guest.

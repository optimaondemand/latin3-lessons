📚 PART 1 · THE EIGHT WORDS 12 min

Module 14 vocabulary

LatinMeaningNote
amīcitia, -ae (f.)friendship→ Eng. amicable, amity
fidēs, -eī (f.)faith, trust, loyalty5th decl.; → fidelity, confide
senectūs, -ūtis (f.)old age3rd decl.; the theme of De Senectute
mors, mortis (f.)death3rd decl.; → mortal, mortuary
recordor, -ārīto recall, recollectdeponent — passive form, active sense
iūcundus, -a, -umpleasant, delightful→ the noun iūcunditās, “pleasantness”
cōnstāns, cōnstantissteadfast, consistent, resolute3rd decl. adj.; → constant, constancy
sapiēns, sapientis (m.)a wise person, philosopher→ sapient, homo sapiens
💡 TWO TO WATCH

fidēs is a 5th-declension noun — its genitive -eī is the giveaway. recordor is deponent: you will see it as recordābātur (“he was recalling”) in both passages tomorrow. Do not read the passive endings as passive meaning.

📌 PART 2 · WORDS IN PHRASES 10 min

Seeing them at work

Read each phrase, find the module word, and note the construction around it. These are the exact chunks you will meet in the full sentences tomorrow.

Nihil est amīcitiā praestabilius…

“Nothing is more excellent than friendship.” — amīcitiā ablative of comparison after praestabilius (comparative).

…sine virtūte amīcitia esse nōn potest.

“…without virtue friendship cannot exist.” — amīcitia nom. subject; esse…potest complementary infinitive.

Nōn est senectūs pertimēscenda.

“Old age is not to be greatly feared.” — senectūs nom. subject of a passive periphrastic.

Sapiēns senectūtem nōn timet sed expectat.

“The wise person does not fear old age but awaits it.” — sapiēns nom. subject; senectūtem acc. object.

💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · no wrong answer
Which of these eight words do you expect to trip you up tomorrow?

Commit first: iūcundus gives Cicero the noun iūcunditās. Guess what iūcunditātem vīvendī means, then reveal.

“The pleasantness of living.” iūcunditātem = accusative of the abstract noun “pleasantness”; vīvendī = genitive of the gerund “of living.” Cicero calls friendship aptum ad iūcunditātem vīvendī — “suited to the pleasantness of living.”

💡 THE TWO THAT TRIP READERS

Result vs. purpose: both use ut + subjunctive; the giveaway for result is an anticipating word (tam, tantus, ita) in the main clause. Characteristic vs. ordinary relative: the ordinary relative takes the indicative; the characteristic clause takes the subjunctive and means “the sort of person who…”.

📚 STUDIA HŪMĀNITĀTIS

The phrase studia hūmānitātis — “the pursuits of humane letters” — is the heart of tomorrow's passage and the ancestor of our word humanities. Cicero argues these studia are not luxuries but the very substance of civilization. Keep the phrase in mind; it is the passage's thesis.

✅ TRY IT · vocabulary
Perfugium in the phrase adversīs perfugium praebent means:

Commit, then reveal: what single test tells a result clause apart from a purpose clause when both use ut + subjunctive? Answer, then check.

Look in the main clause for an anticipating word — tam, ita, tantus, sīc, adeō. If one is present, the ut-clause is almost always result (“so … that”). A negative result uses ut nōn, whereas negative purpose uses nē — that negation is a second, confirming test.

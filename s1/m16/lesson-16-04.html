⏰ PART 1 · THE PASSAGE 16 min

Pro Archiā Poētā — near-original, §§18–26

This passage is minimally adapted. Only the most essential words are glossed. Read each line aloud, find the main verb, map every subordinate clause, then translate.

CICERO, PRO ARCHIĀ POĒTĀ — MINIMALLY ADAPTED
1.Etenim omnēs artēs quae ad hūmānitātem pertinent habent quoddam commūne vinculum et quasi cognātiōne quādam inter sē continentur. 2.Nōn erat difficile quī in eā essent condiciōne et dignitāte ut patriam dēsiderāre possent. 3.Haec studia adulēscentiam alunt, senectūtem oblectant, secundās rēs ornant, adversīs perfugium ac sōlācium praebent. 4.Quod sī nōn hic tantus frūctus ostenderētur et sī ex hīs studiīs dēlectātiō sōla peterētur, tamen hanc animī remissiōnem hūmānissimam ac līberālissimam iūdicārētis. 5.Nam illa quae saepe memorat Archias, cum ipse sōlācium requīrit, recordātur — ita in animum penetrant ut nusquam discēdant. 6.An vērō tam parvī animī nostrae erant virtūtēs ut, quod dē Pompēiō fēcimus, hoc idem dē Archiā scrīberēmus? 7.Sī quis minōre glōriā cōntentus esset, tamen hōs poētae suāvitātem versuum laudāre dēbēret. 8.Poēta autem nōn erat quī nōn aliquod testimōnium ingeniī relīquisset — Archias relīquit.
Gloss: cognātiōne — kinship · condiciōne et dignitāte — in position and rank · oblectant — delight · adversīs — in adversity (dat./abl. pl. used as noun) · perfugium — refuge · remissiōnem — relaxation · hūmānissimam ac līberālissimam — most humane and most befitting a free person · penetrant — pierce, penetrate · tam parvī animī — so petty of spirit (genitive of quality) · suāvitātem versuum — sweetness of his verses · relīquisset = pluperf. subj. (relative clause of characteristic, secondary sequence)
📚 PART 2 · LITERARY CONTEXT 10 min
🏛️ PRO ARCHIĀ (62 BC)

Pro Archiā is Cicero's defense of the Greek poet Archias, whose Roman citizenship was disputed. It is as much a defense of the studia hūmānitātis — humane letters — as of the man. Cicero's argument: poetry and philosophy are not luxuries but the substance of what makes a civilization. The speech became, in the Renaissance, a foundational text for the defense of liberal education. After a semester of Plautus, Petronius, and Cicero's political writing, this text asks you to hear Cicero reflecting on why literature itself matters.

⚖️ THE FAMOUS LINE

Line 3 — Haec studia adulēscentiam alunt, senectūtem oblectant, secundās rēs ornant, adversīs perfugium ac sōlācium praebent — is among the most quoted sentences in Latin prose: “These studies nourish the young, delight the old, adorn prosperity, and offer refuge and solace in adversity.” Note the four parallel clauses, each verb final, building by tricolon and beyond — Ciceronian architecture in miniature.

Commit, then reveal: translate line 3 — Haec studia adulēscentiam alunt, senectūtem oblectant, secundās rēs ornant, adversīs perfugium ac sōlācium praebent. Write your version, then check.

“These studies nourish the young, delight the old, adorn prosperity, and provide refuge and solace in adversity.” — Four parallel clauses, each with its own accusative object and a verb held to the end (alunt … oblectant … ornant … praebent). Adversīs is a dative/ablative plural used as a noun (“for those in adversity”). The accumulating parallelism is what makes the line ring.

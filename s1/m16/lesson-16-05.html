📝 PART 3 · THREE FINALS-REHEARSAL SENTENCES 16 min

Commit, then reveal

These three are drawn straight from the Pro Archiā passage — the hardest syntax in it. Translate each in the box before you reveal. This is your last rehearsal before the graded Translation Assignment.

1. Etenim omnēs artēs quae ad hūmānitātem pertinent habent quoddam commūne vinculum et quasi cognātiōne quādam inter sē continentur.

For all the arts which belong to human culture have a certain common bond and are, as it were, held together by a kind of kinship with each other. — quae … pertinent = ordinary relative clause, indicative. continentur = present passive. quasi … quādam = double hedging (quasi = “as if / as it were”; quādam = “a certain kind of”) — Cicero marking this as metaphor.

2. Quod sī nōn hic tantus frūctus ostenderētur et sī ex hīs studiīs dēlectātiō sōla peterētur, tamen hanc animī remissiōnem hūmānissimam ac līberālissimam iūdicārētis.

But if this great benefit were not being demonstrated, and if only pleasure were sought from these studies, still you would judge this relaxation of the mind to be most humane and most befitting free people. — quod sī = “but if” (Ciceronian connective). ostenderētur / peterētur / iūdicārētis = present contrary-to-fact (present subj.) — the world of the sentence is one where this IS in fact happening, so the condition is contrary to fact.

3. Poēta autem nōn erat quī nōn aliquod testimōnium ingeniī relīquisset — Archias relīquit.

But there was no poet who had not left some testimony to his genius — Archias left one. — nōn erat quī nōn = double-negative characteristic clause (“there was no one such that he had not…” = everyone had). relīquisset = pluperf. subj. in secondary-sequence characteristic clause. The short follow-up — Archias relīquit — breaks from the period for emphasis.
